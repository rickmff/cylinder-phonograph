Cylinder Phonograph

An Edison wax-cylinder phonograph in your browser. Sound is cut into a groove of numbers, the GPU carves those numbers into wax, and a tiny sapphire ball plays them back. No recording of a real phonograph is used: every crackle and wobble comes from the simulation.

Features

  • Record from the microphone, or drop an audio file to cut it onto a blank
  • Wind the spring with the crank and start it with the lever
  • Lift the reproducer and drop it anywhere on the cylinder
  • Grab the cylinder and scratch it, forwards and backwards
  • 3D sound: the horn is the source, the camera is your head
  • Zoom until one groove fills the screen: what you see is what you hear
  • One HTML file, WebGL2 and Web Audio, no libraries

How it works

One loop runs 48,000 times a second. The clockwork says where the needle is; recording writes a depth there, playback reads it back. Below, each part names the equation behind it and says in one line how the code does it.

1Where the needle is

Spiral

Helix

A screw with 100 threads per inch moves the needle one groove per turn, so a position is just “turns since the start”.

Groove samples

Sampling

Each turn holds 4,096 depths. Recording averages into them and playback reads between them, so any speed and direction work.

2Recording

Horn

Biquad high-pass · feedback comb

Two filters remove the bass a horn can’t hold. A 2.45 ms echo, one trip along a 42 cm horn and back, adds its honk.

Diaphragm

Resonant low-pass

A filter that rings at 2.3 kHz, like the glass disc.

Cutter

tanh soft clipping

Loud sound is squashed smoothly, so the cut never goes past ±44 µm.

Wax

Box filter · random noise

Each groove cell keeps the average of the sound that passed over it, plus a faint grain you later hear as hiss.

Long files

Speed scaling

A file that doesn’t fit is trimmed, or cut slower, down to 120 rpm, as early cylinders were.

3The record is an array

Depth table

Float32Array

4,096 × 371 numbers in micrometres. The GPU draws it, the needle plays it, and your browser saves it.

Wear

Random impulses

The built-in cylinder gets 300 dust specks and three scratches, so it clicks once per turn.

4Drawing the groove

Groove shape

Displacement mapping

Close up, a fine mesh is wrapped around the cylinder and pushed in by the depths. Each groove is a curved channel 163 µm wide.

Smooth zoom

Catmull–Rom spline

Depths are joined by a smooth curve, so at extreme zoom you see hills, not steps.

From afar

Level of detail

Far away, each groove fades to its average depth, so 369 grooves don’t shimmer.

Light

PBR (GGX) · AgX

Physically based light, clear-coat wax, soft shadows, ambient occlusion and bloom.

5Playback

Ball in the groove

Circle–parabola contact

The 70 µm ball rests on the groove walls; in deep cuts it can’t reach the bottom.

Tiny dips

Morphological dilation

The ball can’t fit into dips smaller than itself, so it rides over them, as on real records.

Jumps

Newton’s second law

The reproducer’s weight can pull the needle down only so fast. When the groove drops faster, the needle flies for a moment and buzzes.

Sound

Velocity pickup

What you hear is how fast the needle moves, not where it is.

Reproducer

Biquad filters

The diaphragm rings at 2 kHz, its weight dulls the highs, and the wooden box adds warmth at 480 Hz.

Room

Schroeder reverb

Four echo loops and two diffusers make a small room.

Peaks

tanh soft clipping

Very loud moments are rounded off instead of clipping.

Needle talk

High-pass filter

The needle’s own scratchy sound, heard only when the camera is right next to it.

6Sound in space

Distance

Inverse distance law

Twice as far, half as loud.

Direction

Directivity cone

The horn beams forward: off its axis it gets quieter.

Brightness

Off-axis low-pass

High notes beam the most, so walking around the horn makes it duller.

Ears

HRTF

The browser’s head-related filters place the sound around your head. Best with headphones.

Clicks

Decaying sine · noise

Lever, crank, lid and key clicks are synthesized on the spot and placed where the part is.

7Clockwork

Governor

First-order lag

Speed eases toward 160 rpm: about half a second to start, a third to brake.

Spring

Linear drain

A full wind lasts 1,100 turns, about 7 minutes. Each crank turn adds 7 %, and below 12 % the music sags.

Wobble

Sine modulation

Tiny speed wobbles, slow and fast, that grow as the spring runs out.

Your hand

Damped spring

Scratching pulls the cylinder through a stiff spring while the belt slips, and the groove is read backwards and forwards.

Moving the needle

Damped spring

The carriage follows your pointer, then locks into the nearest groove.

8Volume

Knob

Square law · low-pass

Turning it down makes the sound quieter and duller at once, like a damper in the horn. Past three quarters it overdrives.

The cylinder

In the Hall of the Mountain King, from Grieg’s own piano arrangement, in a public-domain score from the Mutopia Project. The score is read as text, never run. A small wavetable piano plays it, speeding up as Grieg wrote it, and it is cut exactly like your recordings.
